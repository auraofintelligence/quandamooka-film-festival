Start a film idea without needing to know everything yet.

Move one small step at a time. A good plan names the spark, the people, the place, the permission questions, and the next useful action.

First-Time Path

From story seed to readiness.

Idea

Write the first rough thought in your own words. Do not polish too early.

Story seed

Name the person, place, question, feeling, or event that gives the film life.

People, place, and permissions

Ask who is involved, who can say yes, and what should stay private.

Outline

Sketch the beginning, middle, ending, and the moments that make the story move.

Storyboard

Use rough frames, sketches, photos, or AI prompt drafts to test the feel of scenes.

Shot list

Decide what to film, where to stand, what sound you need, and what backup shots help.

Assets

List the phone, mic, tripod, screen, skill, helper, file space, or shared kit you can use, borrow, or need to protect.

Production plan

Write down people, gear, time, transport, weather, safety, files, and review points.

Review

Watch the plan with fresh eyes. Check claims, dignity, consent, cultural care, and privacy.

Readiness

When the film can be shared safely, prepare links, captions, credits, permissions, and notes.

Useful Builders

Start with these three forms.

Story Seed

Capture the spark, why it matters, who is involved, and what stays private.

Character / Place

Describe people, groups, places, and objects with care before filming.

Script Outline

Turn the idea into scenes without locking the story too early.

Asset Share

Map what can be used together, who stewards it, and what stays private.

Novice Friendly

Simple is not shallow.

A phone film can still have a strong source trail, clear permissions, useful captions, and a thoughtful review path. A big documentary can still start with one honest paragraph.

Before you film, ask:

  • Who is in charge of this story?
  • Who needs to be asked before filming or sharing?
  • What music, images, or footage do we own or have permission to use?
  • What should stay private until reviewed?
